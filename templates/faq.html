🇨🇦 Getting Started in Canada
I just moved to Canada. How do I use public transit?
Welcome to Canada! 🎉 Public transit here varies by city, but the basics are the same everywhere:

Step 1: Find out which agency runs transit in your city (e.g. TTC in Toronto, STM in Montreal, TransLink in Vancouver).
Step 2: Get a transit card. In Ontario, it's called a PRESTO card — you tap it on buses and subways instead of paying cash. Other cities have similar cards (Opus in Montreal, Compass in Vancouver).
Step 3: Download the Transit app (transitapp.com) — it shows real-time arrivals for every city in Canada and is completely free.

You can ask TransitGPT about any agency, route, or stop in Canada!
What is a PRESTO card and where do I get one?
A PRESTO card is a reusable transit card used across Ontario (TTC, GO Transit, OC Transpo, and many more). Think of it like a tap-to-pay card just for transit.

You can get one at:
  • Any subway station in Toronto
  • Shoppers Drug Mart locations
  • Online at prestocard.ca
The card costs $6 and you load money onto it. Montreal uses the Opus card and Vancouver uses the Compass card — same idea, different names.
🚌 Travelling Between Cities
How do I travel from Toronto to Montreal?
Great question! Here are all your options:

✈️ By Plane — Fastest option (~1.5 hrs). Check Air Canada, WestJet, or Porter Airlines.

🚆 By Train — VIA Rail runs daily trains between Toronto Union Station and Montreal Central Station (~5 hrs). Book at viarail.ca.

🚌 By Intercity Bus — Several options:
  • FlixBus — flixbus.ca (affordable, book online)
  • Megabus — ca.megabus.com (budget-friendly)
  • Coach Canada — coachcanada.com
🚗 By Rideshare (carpooling) — Very popular on this route!
  • KangRide — kangride.com (Canadian carpooling app)
  • PopaRide — poparide.com (connect with drivers heading the same way)
  • BlaBlaCar — blablacar.ca
🚗 By Car — About 5.5 hrs via Highway 401.

How do I travel from Vancouver to Victoria?
Victoria is on Vancouver Island, so you'll need to cross the water!

⛴️ By Ferry (most popular) — BC Ferries runs from Tsawwassen terminal (near Vancouver) to Swartz Bay terminal (near Victoria). The ferry ride is about 1.5 hrs and very scenic. Book at bcferries.com.

✈️ By Plane — Harbour Air runs seaplanes between downtown Vancouver and downtown Victoria (~35 mins). Check harbourair.com.

Once in Victoria, BC Transit operates local buses. Check bctransit.com.

🚇 Local Transit in Major Cities
How do I use the subway in Toronto?
Toronto's subway is run by the TTC (Toronto Transit Commission). There are 4 lines:
  • Line 1 (Yonge-University) — the main U-shaped line through downtown
  • Line 2 (Bloor-Danforth) — runs east-west across the city
  • Line 3 (Ontario) — newer extension to Scarborough
  • Line 4 (Sheppard) — short line in north Toronto
Pay with a PRESTO card (tap on entry), tokens, or cash at station booths. One fare covers transfers to TTC buses and streetcars within 2 hours.

📱 Download the TTC app or use ttc.ca for maps and schedules.

How do I use the metro in Montreal?
Montreal's metro is run by the STM (Société de transport de Montréal) and has 4 lines:
  • Green line — east-west through downtown
  • Orange line — the main north-south line
  • Yellow line — connects downtown to the South Shore
  • Blue line — serves the university area (Université de Montréal)
Get an Opus card at any metro station to pay. Single fares, daily passes, and monthly passes are all available.

📱 Visit stm.info for maps and schedules.

How does the SkyTrain work in Vancouver?
The SkyTrain is Vancouver's rapid transit system, operated by TransLink. It's elevated (above ground) and fully automated — no driver!

There are 3 lines:
  • Expo Line — downtown Vancouver to Surrey
  • Millennium Line — connects Burnaby and Coquitlam
  • Canada Line — connects downtown to YVR airport and Richmond
Pay with a Compass card (tap in AND tap out — very important or you get charged the maximum fare). You can also take TransLink buses and the SeaBus ferry with the same card.

📱 Visit translink.ca for trip planning.

🚗 Rideshare & Carpooling in Canada
What is KangRide and how do I use it?
KangRide (kangride.com) is a Canadian carpooling platform where drivers heading between cities offer seats to passengers for a shared cost. It's very popular for routes like Toronto → Ottawa, Toronto → Montreal, and many others.

How it works:
  • Create a free account at kangride.com
  • Search for your route and date
  • Book a seat with a driver going your way
  • Pay a small fee (much cheaper than the bus or train)
It's safe, affordable, and a great way to meet people in Canada!
What is PopaRide?
PopaRide (poparide.com) is another Canadian ridesharing app — especially popular in Quebec and Ontario. Drivers post trips and passengers can request a seat.

It's great for intercity travel where buses are infrequent or expensive. Prices are set by drivers and are usually very reasonable.

Sign up at poparide.com — it's free to join.

Is transit free for seniors or students in Canada?
Discounts vary by city and agency, but here are common ones:

Students: Most agencies offer discounted monthly passes with a valid student ID. Ask your school's student union — many universities have U-Pass programs that include unlimited transit in the tuition fee.

Seniors (65+): Most agencies offer discounted or free rides during off-peak hours. For example, TTC offers a Senior discount fare.

Children: Children under 12 ride free on most Canadian transit systems.

Check your local agency's website for current discount programs.
Can I take transit to the airport in Canada?
Yes! Most major Canadian airports have transit connections:

  • Toronto (YYZ) — UP Express train from Union Station (~25 min), or TTC bus routes
  • Montreal (YUL) — STM Bus 747 runs 24/7 between downtown and the airport
  • Vancouver (YVR) — Canada Line SkyTrain goes directly to the airport from downtown (~26 min)
  • Ottawa (YOW) — OC Transpo Route 97 connects to the airport
  • Calgary (YYC) — CTrain Red Line connects to the airport
